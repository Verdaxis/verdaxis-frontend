# Verdaxis Frontend — Impeccable Context

## Product Frame
Verdaxis is a maritime alternative fuels trading and intelligence platform.
The frontend has two surfaces that must still feel like one product:
- Public site: category education, market narrative, trust-building, product explanation
- Authenticated app: operational trading, market surveillance, portfolio and fleet workflows

The product should feel like a serious market operating system for a niche, high-value industrial market.
It must not feel like a generic SaaS admin, a crypto toy terminal, or a marketing-first startup template.

## Audience
Primary audiences:
- Fuel suppliers posting and managing executable asks
- Buyers browsing, filtering, and hitting executable asks or managing bids
- Commercial users who need confidence, traceability, and speed more than novelty

These users are time-constrained and commercially sensitive.
They need interfaces that feel credible, legible, and information-dense without looking chaotic.

## Core Tone
Use these words as the standard:
- operational
- credible
- exact
- restrained
- market-native
- maritime-industrial

Avoid these tones:
- playful
- whimsical
- consumer-social
- crypto-hype
- luxury-editorial
- ultra-dark gamer-terminal

## Visual North Star
Verdaxis should look like:
- a modern maritime market console
- a product where benchmark, qualification, and execution context matter
- a system designed for signal reading and action

The one thing users should remember:
- Verdaxis feels like a purpose-built alternative-fuels market terminal, not a generic listings board.

## Non-Negotiables
1. Information hierarchy must stay clear under dense market data.
2. Qualification context must be fast to scan.
3. Interactive trading surfaces must look actionable, not decorative.
4. Public and app surfaces may differ in pacing, but must share the same product identity.
5. Components must feel designed, not default.

## Layout Principles
- Prefer strong section structure over floating card soup.
- Use compact density in app surfaces, but preserve breathing room around key actions.
- Filter rails should feel like a market workspace, not a search form.
- Tables and orderbook surfaces should privilege alignment, comparison, and legibility.
- Empty states should explain the market condition or filter condition, not just absence.

## Typography
- Keep the existing Verdaxis stack unless a deliberate future redesign replaces it globally.
- Favor crisp hierarchy and compact weights over oversized display type in the app.
- Tiny support text should be used sparingly; avoid dropping below the point where scanning becomes fragile.
- Numbers, prices, quantities, and benchmark deltas should read cleanly and consistently.

## Color System Guidance
- Base palette should remain sober: slate/ink neutrals, maritime greens, controlled alerts.
- Green means executable-positive / live / buy-side favorable states, not generic success everywhere.
- Red should communicate ask-side / caution / unfavorable price direction clearly.
- Amber is for warnings, off-spec, crossed states, or qualification flags.
- Do not introduce trendy neon palettes, purple gradients, or over-saturated accent systems.

## Component Rules
- Use shared Verdaxis controls and panel primitives wherever possible.
- Dropdowns, pills, tabs, filters, and tables must feel like one system.
- A control representing an aggregate scope must not visually imply a filtered scope.
- Tabs sharing one workspace should preserve context unless the product explicitly breaks it.
- Tooltips may supplement dense data, but they cannot be the only practical path to critical understanding forever.

## Motion
- Motion should be subtle and purposeful.
- Prioritize hover clarity, disclosure transitions, and state feedback.
- Avoid decorative looping effects.
- Keep app motion tighter and more restrained than public marketing motion.

## Public Site Guidance
- Public pages can be more spacious and narrative, but must still feel industrial and credible.
- Do not use startup-generic hero clichés, vague abstract blobs, or overused AI gradients.
- The public experience should make Verdaxis feel trusted and specific to maritime fuels.

## Authenticated App Guidance
- The app should feel denser, more instrumental, and more market-native than the public site.
- Market surfaces should privilege comparability and action over ornament.
- Watchlist, orderbook, listings, and intelligence views should read as parts of one trading environment.

## Anti-Patterns
Do not ship:
- default browser-looking controls in primary workflows
- purple-on-dark or purple-on-white visual drift
- random card mosaics with no structural rhythm
- full-width whitespace-heavy empty shells in the app
- consumer wishlist language unless the feature is explicitly consumer-like
- faux-terminal styling that harms readability
- mixing aggregate counts with filtered counts without clear distinction
- hiding critical market qualification context behind weak affordances

## Decision Guardrails
When evaluating a UI change, prefer the option that:
- improves scan speed
- preserves market credibility
- keeps the system visually coherent across pages
- reduces ambiguity in scope, state, and action
- feels product-designed rather than library-default

Reject a change if it makes Verdaxis feel more like:
- a generic SaaS dashboard
- a classifieds/listings marketplace with no market intelligence layer
- a flashy fintech demo detached from real operations
